## Design Context

### Users
Researchers (often students) who blind-score fluorescence images of identified neurons in C. elegans,
usually one person, in long sessions of tens to hundreds of images on a Windows or Mac desktop. They
need to judge faint cells reliably, keep their place, never lose an answer, and trust that the tool
keeps them blind (no genotype, file name or brightness number ever shown while scoring).

### Brand Personality
Calm, precise, trustworthy. Like a good lab instrument: quiet, exact, nothing flashy. The images are
the star; the interface steps back. Emotional goal: confidence that the result will stand up in a paper.

### Aesthetic Direction
Keep the current look (dark scoring page tuned for judging dim cells; home screen in light or dark).
Refine rather than redesign: consistent tokens, spacing and states. No decorative gradients, glow,
glassmorphism or accent stripes. The app runs offline, so no web fonts are loaded from the internet.

### Design Principles
1. The image comes first: nothing on screen competes with or tints the image area.
2. Never lose work and never surprise: every action says what happened; risky steps ask first.
3. Blind by construction: no UI element may reveal genotype, file names, order of addition or numbers.
4. WCAG AA and full keyboard use: visible focus, labels, contrast, reduced motion respected.
5. Plain, short wording; no jargon the scorer does not need.
